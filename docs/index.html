EspRangeTest

map two fingers to move
Flash two boards, leave one behind, and walk away with the other plus this page.

Connect to either board — each one reports what it can hear, so the numbers are for the link between them. Tap Connect a board again to add the second one.

For a range walk, drop one board, tap set base where you left it, turn on GPS, and walk. Every packet gets pinned to where you were standing when it arrived, so bare track is ground where nothing got through.

A board with a GNSS module draws its own track, and every packet to or from it is pinned to where it was instead. Connect to any board - the one with the GNSS or one that heard it - and the track fills in from whatever each has seen; a board that was out of reach sends what it stockpiled once you reconnect.

field turns one link into a coverage map: pick the two boards, the protocol and a stretch of time when nothing but position changed, and the packets heard and missed are spread into colour between and around where they landed. trace lays what it was made from over the top, and bearing lets a walk out in one direction stand in for the others.

With a base set, the field is drawn through a fit of signal against range, so a few spokes describe the whole disc. height takes out how high a GNSS end was flying - low down the ground itself is in the way, so 900 m along the ground beats 1.1 km at 100 m up - and the at slider says which height the map answers for. leftovers then shows only what the fit could not explain, which is where hills and buildings are. Use it on delivery: a shadow deep enough to matter kills the link, and a dead link reports no signal at all, so only the packets that failed to arrive can show it.

Needs Chrome on Android or desktop. Web Bluetooth won't work from a file:// page — use the GitHub Pages URL, or python3 -m http.server and open http://localhost:8000.
